# Film brief — Signal / Route / Resolve

## Objective

Make Roster immediately legible as a local priority router for agent tools. The film should feel like an editorial product trailer rather than a dashboard walkthrough.

## Audience

Developers and agent builders who understand terminals, MCP tools, and the pain of too many capabilities competing for context.

## Narrative

`200 tools` is the pressure. A real-looking terminal receives `verify checkout flow`. Roster listens locally, converts intent into a route, compares three relevant capabilities, selects Playwright, returns a resolved outcome, and stores a compact local signal. The ending widens into a calm orbit of agent-facing integrations.

## Visual direction

Near-black graphite, cream editorial type, a restrained warm coral signal, and a small mint success state. Each beat has one focal object. Curved route rails are quiet glass paths, not neon lines. The supplied Roster mark is used as a bright mark on black.

## Factual claims shown

- `200` is shown as the current launch framing used by the existing film brief.
- The command is illustrative: `roster route "verify checkout flow"`.
- The route is shown as local and ranked by fit, reliability, and outcome.
- Playwright is selected for a verification task; this is a creative example, not a benchmark claim.
- The end command is `npx roster init`.

## Audio direction

The new score is generated locally from deterministic oscillators, plucks, ticks, impacts, and a short noise sweep. It uses the same contrast as the picture: a low warm pulse, crisp terminal-like ticks, a click/resolve accent, then air for the final orbit. It contains no speech or commercial music.

## Future refinements

Replace the procedural score with a commissioned/licensed track, add a real terminal capture only if it can remain crisp and deterministic, and align the final orbit mark list with the exact public MCP catalogue at launch.
